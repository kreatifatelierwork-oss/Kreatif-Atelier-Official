## 1. The problem with every idea so far
The orrery, the House of Light, the paper book, the prism, the dewdrops and the glassworks all tell the same story:
*"Kreatif Atelier is a holding company with five companies under it."* That is the org chart, not the story.

The content says something much bigger, and it says it in their own words:
- "Where Strategy Meets Story. Where Story Becomes Legacy."
- "We build brands that *actually move people.*"
- "Great creative work is not decoration — it is a business tool."
- "Every great brand started with a single conversation."
- Ideas | People | Impact. Disciplines today. A brighter tomorrow.

These describe a **process with a direction**: a conversation becomes an idea; the idea gets a strategy; the strategy
becomes a story; the story moves people; people carry it into the future; what lasts becomes a legacy.

The five verticals, the services, the process steps, the industries and the founders are all *part of that journey*.
None of them is the journey itself.

## 2. The idea in one sentence
**The website is a film about how an idea becomes a legacy, the making-of documentary of one idea, shot as a single
luminous take. It plays like a movie, is dense with real technical information, and responds to you at every moment:
your cursor is a lens and a light, your movement conducts the score, and in the end you are cast in the next scene.**

**Why this is the right story:**
- It is **their positioning line made into a plot**: Strategy (Act II) meets Story (Act III); Story becomes Legacy
  (Act VII).
- It uses the whole live site (process, services, industries, founders, CTA) and the new chart (verticals, serves,
  impact, vision) as plot points.
- The holding company is the *reveal*, not the premise. You watch an idea need strategy, story, reach, people and
  futures, and only at the end see that one company holds all five.

## 4. Four signature mechanics

### ① Watch or Direct (the site is a movie you can also play)
- **Watch:** one press of ▶ and the film plays itself start to finish (about 3½ minutes), with captions like
  subtitles.
- **Direct:** scroll, drag or swipe to scrub like a media player, forwards and backwards. The pattern comes from
  GQ × AP "The Extraordinary Lab", where the whole experience is one scrubbable track.
- **Editor keys:** `J / K / L` rewind, pause and forward like a film editor, `Space` plays or pauses, and `1–7` jump
  to acts.
- The act timeline and timecode we already built become the film's scrubber.

**Differences from GQ × AP "The Extraordinary Lab"** (the nearest precedent):
- We use no dialogue by default.
- We don't use their waveform scrubber or word-by-word karaoke subtitles. Our scrubber is the colour-segment act
  timeline we already built, and captions appear line by line.
- The film is driven by the Lens, the visitor's changing role and the particle modes, none of which AP has.

### ② The Lens (strategy under story, everywhere)
- Your cursor carries a soft circular **lens**. Through it you see the *other layer* of the same frame: over a
  finished render you see its blueprint (wireframe, measurements, labels); over a blueprint you see the finished
  render.
- It is "Where Strategy Meets Story" as a physical interaction, available the entire film.
- The references show it works: Lando Norris's cursor reveals the helmet over the face, Hubtown reveals geometry,
  Opal's dot-matrix lights up under the cursor, and Apple shows the AirPods x-ray. Nobody has made it the *thesis*.
- Phones: press and hold to open the lens under your thumb.

### ③ You are cast (responsiveness with meaning)
Your pointer changes role in each act:
- a second light in the cold open;
- a force that stirs the Idea in Act I;
- the lens in Act II;
- a campaign launcher in Act IV;
- a person breathing with the crowd in Act V;
- a spark in Act VI;
- the other half of the conversation in the post-credits scene.

Every channel responds, as one physics:

| Channel | What it does |
|---|---|
| Pointer position | Particles and light |
| Pointer speed | Shimmer and sound |
| Click | Impulse waves |
| Scroll speed | Motion blur and a whoosh |
| Idle | After a few seconds the film breathes and the HUD shows "HOLD" |
| Hyderabad local time | Sets the light temperature (dawn, day, dusk, night) |
| Phone tilt | Parallax, with permission |
| Tab hidden | The score fades out |

### ④ A score you conduct (sound as a layer)
The music is **generative, built in the browser with Web Audio**. No licensed audio is needed, and composed stems can
replace it later.

**Per act:**
- a harmonic bed that changes chord per act;
- each vertical brings its own voice when it enters:
  - Aurelius: low, brass-like;
  - GGP: a projector hum plus strings;
  - Valora: plucked;
  - Rudra Yugen: digital arpeggio;
  - Asraya: breath and chimes.
- At Legacy all five voices resolve into **one chord**: "one vision", heard.

**You play it:**
- cursor height sets the filter;
- left and right set the stereo pan;
- speed adds shimmer;
- clicks strike tones in the act's key;
- hovering buttons gives soft ticks.

**Rules:**
- Sound starts only after the gate choice, and a mute toggle is always visible.
- Scrubbing crossfades states and never speeds the music up (the AP Lab lesson).
- Reduced motion keeps the bed and drops the whooshes.

**Optional later:** a voice-over reading the real copy, as Sleep Well does. This needs a voice artist.

## 5. "Technologically information-heavy", without inventing a single fact
The frame is a fine-line FUI (the fictional user interfaces of film) that is dense, precise and readable. **Every
number and label on it is real.**

**Film furniture:**
- act and timecode;
- frame guides;
- focal length and focus distance of the actual camera;
- render stats: the live frame rate and the number of particles on screen;
- the lens readout.

**Content as documentation:**
- process step numbers;
- service names;
- each house's "what we do", as callouts attached to the geometry (our proven annotation system).

**Big "spec" numbers that are true** (like Apple's and Zentry's stat posters, but counted from our own content):
5 houses · 6 services · 5 process steps · 13 industries · 4 founders · 1 vision.

**Live data about *you*, never stats about clients:**
- watch time;
- cursor distance;
- clicks;
- sound level (a real meter);
- Hyderabad time.

Act IV's "analytics console" shows *your session*, and says so. Terminal Industries does the honest version of
this for its product demo, labelling it "Demonstration data". It's the honest version of every agency's
fake-dashboard hero.

All of it is real HTML text; the canvas is decoration.

## 6. The look
- **Not space, and no planets.** A boundless **pearl studio** (palette A, the default) or **ink studio** (palette B),
  with the Idea as the only subject.
- Each act is a **mode** of the same world and the same particles, so the film never cuts:

| Act | Mode |
|---|---|
| I | Raw light |
| II | Blueprint |
| III | Cinema |
| IV | Flow map |
| V | Constellations |
| VI | Lattice |
| VII | Monument |

- **Colour has a job:**
  - gold = the Idea;
  - each vertical's colour appears *only* when that house acts on the Idea;
  - cool ink = strategy lines;
  - warm light = story.
- **Type:**
  - Outfit for act titles and headlines;
  - Cormorant italic for the spoken lines (the captions);
  - Manrope for microtype;
  - **one new monospace for the HUD** (PROPOSED: IBM Plex Mono or JetBrains Mono, both free under the OFL).
- **The monument and the Idea are abstract forms and never a logo**, crest or monogram. No chart icons appear.
- **From dust to monument:** for six acts the Idea is light (particles). At Legacy the particles settle onto a real
  solid surface, and a polished gold form (studio-HDRI reflections, the only fully solid object in the film)
  condenses out of them. Permanence is *earned* visually.
- **Precedent for "one object held while the world changes around it":** the Getty's *Tracing Art* keeps a single
  painting centred while its ownership history, era and surrounding artworks change with a year scrubber. We do the
  same with the Idea and the film clock.

## 7. How it's built (feasible, and lighter than the orrery)
- **Protagonist:**
  - one GPU particle system (about 60k points on desktop, about 15k on phones) simulated on the GPU;
  - it morphs between baked target shapes per act, with curl-noise life;
  - cursor forces and click impulses run in the same shader.
- **Blueprint and Lens:**
  - the same particles and a few guide meshes drawn with a line/blueprint material;
  - a screen-space mask at the cursor mixes the two layers.
- **The film:**
  - our existing timeline `s` becomes the film clock;
  - Watch mode advances it at film speed;
  - keyframes per act live in `lib/config.ts`.
- **Sound:** a small Web Audio engine (oscillators, filtered noise, a generated reverb) scheduled by `s`.
- **Reused from the current build:**
  - server-rendered HTML outline (SEO and screen readers);
  - the deferred stage (Lighthouse mobile 93);
  - timeline, jump menu, reduced motion, keyboard, fallback;
  - palettes, the founder cut-outs and the mailto.
- **Phones:** fewer particles, no depth of field, lens on press-and-hold, and a vertical camera framing per act.
- **Reduced motion:** acts become still frames with crossfades, and Watch mode advances act by act.
- **Weak devices:** the CSS fallback shows the acts as illustrated frames.

**Flow tests** (from `CREATIVITY.md` §2b):
- **Verb fingerprint:**
  - This concept: *choose → meet → watch → stir → look through → roll → launch → breathe → spark → turn → read →
    meet*. That is 11 distinct verbs, every one tied to the story, bookended by the same verb (*meet*).
  - The current orrery build: *scroll → hold → tap → hold → tap → hold → read*, only 3 distinct verbs.
- **Retell test (target):** "You meet a light and it becomes an idea. You watch it get planned, filmed, released to
  thousands and carried by people into the future, until it turns into a golden monument. Then the credits roll and
  it asks you to start the next one."

**Honest risks:**
- **Particle morphing is a known technique.** It stays distinctive only through the modes, the Lens, the real-data
  FUI, the film structure and the sound. Without them it would look generic, so they are not optional extras.
- **The FUI must stay elegant.** It should feel like fine print, not cyberpunk (CLAUDE.md's old warning still applies).
- **Generative sound needs taste and tuning time.** A composer can replace it later.
